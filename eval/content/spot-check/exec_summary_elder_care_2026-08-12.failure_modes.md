# Failure-mode taxonomy — T5 exec_summary spot-check, Elder Care (2026-08-12)

**Scope:** root-cause grouping of the 53 exec_summary claims after operator reconciliation (see `exec_summary_elder_care_2026-08-12.verdicts.yaml`). Read this alongside the operator's notes in `exec_summary_elder_care_2026-08-12.review_queue.md` and the M2 `calibration-disagreement-audit-2026-08-12.md`.

## 0. Method note — the dominant root cause, up front

Before bucketing individual claims: **the single largest driver of "unsupported" drafts (28 of 30) was not a corpus-support failure or a judge-capability failure — it was a retrieval-*index* scoping gap.**

- The exec_summary text is generated from `uc13_ale.analysis.diligence_report.executive_summary`, which is assembled from the per-workstream analysis tables (`business_model`, `kpi`, `financial_trends`, `quality_of_earnings`, `forecast`, `customer_quality`, `legal_contracts`) plus `diligence_report`'s own aggregation fields (`section_ratings_json`, `section_confidence_json`, `top_10_issues_json`, `reconciliation_summary_json`). Every field in those tables carries its own `source_doc`/`source_location` citation back to a VDR file — they are legitimate, independently-sourced evidence, not a restatement of the exec_summary.
- The T5 spot-check tool's retrieval (`uc13_ale.ingestion.embeddings_index`, chunk-level vector search over `ingestion.chunks`) only covers **raw VDR documents**. It has no visibility into the `analysis.*` tables that most exec_summary numbers, ratings, and open items actually trace to. Any claim sourced from a computed/aggregated/rated field (dollar figures already summed, Red/Yellow ratings, confidence rollups, ranked open items) is **structurally invisible** to this retrieval path, regardless of retrieval quality — it will always surface the nearest topically-related raw document instead, which is usually wrong-grain (aggregate vs. line-item) or off-topic entirely.
- A targeted, read-only SQL investigation of `uc13_ale.analysis.diligence_report` and five sibling tables (this session, claims 001–053) found exact-value or verbatim-text confirmation for **28 of the 30 drafted-unsupported claims** and 1 drafted-supported claim was reclassified `contradicted` on the same evidence (claim.026 — see bucket `agent_meta_claim` below). This is consistent with the M2 audit's own finding that claims 017/019/025/026 are "workstream-level synthesis judgments... with no literal single source chunk — chunk-level RAG evidence structurally cannot validate these regardless of retrieval quality" (calibration-disagreement-audit-2026-08-12.md §2.2) — this session shows the fix isn't "accept as permanently unverifiable," it's "verify against the right table."
- Secondary finding: the T5 presentation packet's `source_ref` (`source://uc13_ale.analysis.legal.executive_summary`) points at the **wrong table**. `analysis.legal.executive_summary` is a different, unrelated one-paragraph legal-workstream summary; the actual exec_summary text lives in `analysis.diligence_report.executive_summary`. This is a labeling bug in the packet generator, not a content problem — worth a fast product-track fix so future spot-checks/citations point at the right source.

This reframes the whole bucket table below: most "retrieval_miss" claims are really **retrieval_scope_gap** (wrong index, not wrong ranking within the right index).

## 2. What changed from the draft, in one line

Of the 30 drafted-`unsupported` claims, 28 flip to `supported` once checked against `uc13_ale.analysis.*` instead of (or in addition to) VDR chunk RAG; the 2 that don't (027, 028) are the mandatory-always-flag calibration probes, which were already correctly adjudicated. One drafted-`supported` claim (026) flips to `contradicted` on the same investigation — a genuine, previously-undetected rollup miscount. Net: this spot-check finds the exec_summary itself is **far better grounded than the chunk-RAG-only draft suggested**; the draft's low agreement was overwhelmingly an artifact of checking the wrong evidence store, not a sign of an unreliable generator.
