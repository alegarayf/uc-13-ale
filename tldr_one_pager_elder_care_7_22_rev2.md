## Business Snapshot

Elder Care operates a multi-market in-home personal care platform across six markets — New York City, Long Island, Westchester, New Jersey, Massachusetts, and Connecticut — delivering hourly caregiving (4–7.5 hours and 8+ hours), live-in caregiver arrangements, and specialized services including dementia and couples care. The company serves elderly and disabled clients through a W-2 caregiver workforce credentialed primarily as CHHAs and CNAs. As of the week ending 5/09/2025, the active client census stood at 361 (5-week QTD average: 351.8 per the KPI dashboard); the registered caregiver pool totaled 2,123 as of 4/21/2025 (NY: 1,545; MA: 225; NJ: 243; CT: 110). Note that these two figures are drawn from different measurement dates and different data sources — no single document reconciles them to a common period. The platform expanded via the acquisition of Unicity Homecare (NJ-based) in 2023–2024, consolidating operations under the Elder Care brand. The workforce includes onsite caregivers, RN/LPN nurses, care managers, administrative staff, and a Philippines-based offshore function. Revenue is generated through hourly billing and live-in arrangements; services are non-contracted but habitual in nature. No CIM or offering memorandum was identified — this appears to be a non-banked transaction.

## Key Watchouts

- Referral concentration and related-party dependency: Unicity Senior Advisors (affiliated GCM entity) is the single largest referral source, generating 30 new client starts and ~$591,600 in revenue potential in the last 12 months with no formal contract — this revenue stream has no structural protection in a change-of-control and represents a critical diligence gate.

- Caregiver recruiting-to-deployment conversion failure: 27.4% of the 2,123-person caregiver pool has never been deployed; post-hire non-utilization within 2 months reaches 75% in NJ, 69% in CT, and 60% in NYC — indicating that the large headcount figure overstates effective capacity and that recruiting costs may be significantly misallocated.

- Organic vs. acquisition revenue mix is unresolvable: the Unicity Homecare acquisition (2023–2024) has not been disaggregated from organic growth in any available financial document — it is not possible to assess whether the current census or revenue base reflects organic momentum or is acquisition-inflated.

- Service quality at scale and care manager productivity: care manager visit benchmarks (5 visits/10 CM notes per week in NYC; 4 visits/8 CM notes in LI) are below company averages (32 visits/18.9 CM notes company-wide), and client retention/churn data is entirely absent — quality-of-care continuity at the current census level cannot be confirmed.

- Market-level unit economics are opaque: gross margin by market, average hours per client per week, and revenue per caregiver are all missing — it is not possible to determine which markets are profitable, which are subsidized, or whether the multi-market model generates positive incremental returns.

- Payor mix and rate compression risk: bill rates vary materially by payor (e.g., Jewish Family Services NJ at $27/hr vs. standard $36–$42/hr), and payor mix by market is unknown — the revenue per hour and margin profile could differ significantly across markets depending on the proportion of below-market-rate payor contracts.
